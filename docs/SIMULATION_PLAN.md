# How the simulation is set up in AdditiveFOAM

This document links the project README, the four reference papers, and the
AdditiveFOAM source (`external/AdditiveFOAM`, v2.0.0). Every statement about
the solver was checked against its source code; the file is given in
brackets.

## 1. What each paper contributes

| Paper | Process / tool | What we take from it |
|---|---|---|
| Pariona et al. 2018, IJHMT 119 (Al-1.5Fe) | Laser surface remelting, COMSOL | **Closest analogue:** Gaussian laser on Al, Marangoni stress BC, convection + radiation BCs (Eqs. 7–9), 600 µm beam, 20/40/60 mm/s, measured pool width/depth (680/257 µm at 20 mm/s), dσ/dT = −3.5e-4 N/m K. |
| Farzadi et al. 2008, IJAMT 38 (pure Al) | GTA welding, FLUENT, half-domain | **Formulation and properties:** enthalpy-porosity with Carman–Kozeny damping `C(1−fL)²/(fL³+B)` (Eq. 2): this is where the TF's **B = 1e-4** and **"Aroush" = A_mush ≈ 1e8** come from. Linear fL(T) (Eq. 4), symmetry BCs (Eq. 8), complete Al property table, velocity scaling (Eq. 20), G, R, G/R (Eqs. 21–22). |
| Gao et al. 2021, OLT 142 (NiTi DED) | DED, COMSOL + ALE | Transient 3-D model, symmetry half-domain, h = 25 W/m²K + radiation, Marangoni force balance (Eq. 17), and G/R evaluated at the deepest point of a fixed cross-section (Fig. 15). ALE free surface and powder addition are **out of scope** for us. |
| Fuhrich et al. 2001, JLA 13 (steel keyhole) | Keyhole welding, FIDAP | Physics insight: the **sign** of dσ/dT sets the flow direction (outward → wide/shallow; inward → narrow/deep). With a fixed flat surface only dσ/dT matters, not σ₀. Mesh warning: steep gradients behind the source keep sharpening under refinement. |

The README's surface-tension relation `σ = σ0 + dσ/dT (T − Tref)` therefore
enters our model only through `dSigmadT`. Because the surface is flat and
fixed, σ0 and Tref drop out.

## 2. Governing equations, as AdditiveFOAM implements them

| Physics | AdditiveFOAM implementation | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Energy + latent heat | Enthalpy formulation; solid fraction `alpha.solid` from the `thermoPath` table (T → fs), iterated `nThermoCorrectors` times per step | `thermo/TEqn.H`, `utilities/thermoPath` |
| Properties | `kappa`, `Cp` = quadratic polynomials in T for the solid and the liquid, mixed by phase fraction; constant `rho` | `updateProperties.H` |
| Momentum | Incompressible, laminar, Boussinesq buoyancy (`beta`, `rhok`) | `pU/UEqn.H`, `pU/pEqn.H` |
| Mushy-zone damping | `D = ν·180/DAS² · fs²/(1−fs)³`, with fs capped at 0.95 | `pU/UEqn.H` |
| Marangoni | `marangoni` U BC: zero normal velocity, tangential gradient `dU/dn = (dσ/dT/μ)·∇ₛT` | `derivedFvPatchFields/marangoni` |
| Convection + radiation | `mixedTemperature` T BC: `−k∂T/∂n = h(T−T∞) + εσ_SB(T⁴−T∞⁴)` | `derivedFvPatchFields/mixedTemperature` |
| Laser | Volumetric `superGaussian` (k = 2 is a Gaussian) moving along `scanPath`; absorbed power ηP | `movingHeatSource/` |

### Mapping the TF's constants onto AdditiveFOAM

AdditiveFOAM uses DAS (a length) where Farzadi/FLUENT use A_mush. Matching
the Darcy coefficients (`C = 180 μ/DAS²` per unit volume) gives

    DAS = sqrt(180 μ / A_mush) = sqrt(180 · 0.0014 / 1e8) ≈ 50 µm

`scripts/make_cases.py` computes this from `config/baseline.json`.
AdditiveFOAM has no B: it caps the solid fraction at 0.95 instead. That gives
a maximum damping of about 7e3·A_mush, compared with A_mush/B = 1e4·A_mush in
Farzadi's form, so the two are equivalent in practice. If the TF wants
Farzadi's exact form, it is a three-line edit of `pU/UEqn.H`. That would be
our only solver modification, and it needs a rebuild.

### Symmetry plane and laser power

The heat source divides ηP by the analytic beam volume `V0`, and only
re-normalises to the mesh integral when that is within 5% of V0
(`movingHeatSource.C`). With the beam centred on the `y = 0` symmetry plane,
the half-domain integral is about V0/2, so the half-model automatically
receives exactly half the power. **Do not halve the power by hand.**

## 3. Case layout (generated by `scripts/make_cases.py`)

```
z = 0      top: marangoni (U), mixedTemperature h_top + ε (T)
y = 0      symmetry: symmetryPlane (track centreline)
y = 5 mm, x = 0, x = L: sides: noSlip, mixedTemperature h_sides + ε
z = −8 mm  bottom: noSlip, mixedTemperature h_bottom (no radiation)

x: 0 → 18 mm, uniform Δ        laser: x = 3 → 15 mm along y = 0
y: 0 → 2.4 mm uniform Δ, then graded to 0.6 mm at y = 5 mm
z: 0 → −4.8 mm uniform Δ, then graded to 0.6 mm at z = −8 mm
```

| Case | Δ in ROI | Cells |
|---|---|---|
| `cases/mesh_200um` | 200 µm | 56 k |
| `cases/mesh_150um` | 150 µm | 121 k |
| `cases/mesh_100um` | 100 µm | 350 k |

The ROI and domain lengths are multiples of **0.6 mm** (the least common
multiple of 0.2, 0.15 and 0.1 mm), so all three meshes have integer cell
counts with identical block boundaries. A 5 mm ROI is *not* possible at
150 µm (33.3 cells). This is why the ROI is 4.8 mm deep and 2.4 mm in the
half-width (≈ the 5 × 5 mm full-width ROI in the README).

Scan path: dwell at the start with zero power → line at power P and speed v →
laser off for `cooling_time` (cooling and solidification after the beam has
passed).

## 4. Outputs, and how they map to the README's list

| README quantity | Source |
|---|---|
| Temperature field, max T; velocity field, max \|U\| | Time directories (`writeInterval`); `postProcessing/fieldMinMax` every step |
| Time of first melting | First time `max(T) ≥ T_liq` in `fieldMinMax` |
| Pool length / width / depth vs time | `postProcessing/meltPoolDimensions/<iso>.csv` at liquidus and solidus. **Width is the half-width on our half domain: multiply by 2.** |
| Liquid-fraction distribution | `alpha.solid` field (liquid = 1 − alpha.solid) |
| Cooling rate, G, R, G/R, G·R | `solidificationData` (x, y, z, t_s, cooling rate, G vector, v) within the ROI box |
| Flow pattern / vortices | ParaView: U glyphs on cross-sections x = const |

## 5. Run order (validate step by step)

1. **Smoke test:** the unmodified `AMB2018-02-B` tutorial (docs/SETUP.md).
2. **Mesh check:** `blockMesh && checkMesh` on all three cases.
3. **Conduction only:** set `dSigmadT 0` and `beta 0`, which removes both
   flow drivers so U stays zero. Compare the far field with Rosenthal
   (`scripts/estimate_pool.py`), and check the absorbed power: a pool that is
   2× too large usually means the power was halved or doubled by mistake.
   This run is also the "no Marangoni" reference for showing the effect of
   Marangoni flow.
4. **Phase change:** check the pool against the solidus/liquidus isotherms,
   and that `alpha.solid` follows `thermoPath`.
5. **Marangoni on:** the surface flow should be outward (dσ/dT < 0) with two
   symmetric vortices in the cross-section (Pariona Fig. 2e). Peak velocity
   should be O(1 m/s); Farzadi Eq. 20 gives about 2 m/s for the baseline.
6. **Mesh study:** run 200 → 150 → 100 µm with identical inputs.
7. **Optional physics checks:** flip the sign of dσ/dT (Fuhrich), or vary
   the speed over 20/40/60 mm/s (Pariona).

## 6. Mesh-study analysis

- **Compare:** steady pool width, depth, length and cross-section area;
  T_max; |U|_max; centreline cooling rate; G/R at the pool bottom.
- **Refinement ratios:** 1.33 and 1.5 are unequal, so use the generalised
  Richardson/GCI procedure (Celik et al., J. Fluids Eng. 130, 2008), which
  handles non-constant ratios.
- **Robust metrics:** integral quantities (pool area/volume) converge more
  cleanly than point peaks (T_max, |U|_max). Fuhrich notes that peak
  gradients behind the source keep growing under refinement. Report the
  peaks, but only claim grid independence for the metrics whose changes are
  small and monotonic.

## 7. Resolution check: the most important open decision

`scripts/estimate_pool.py` (conduction-only Rosenthal estimate for pure Al at
20 mm/s):

| Absorbed power | Pool width × depth | Cells across depth at 200 / 150 / 100 µm |
|---|---|---|
| 100 W (≈ 2 kW × 5 %, realistic for Al at 1 µm) | 0.22 × 0.11 mm | 0.6 / 0.7 / 1.1 |
| 250 W | 0.54 × 0.27 mm (≈ Pariona's pool) | 1.4 / 1.8 / 2.7 |
| 1000 W (current placeholder) | 2.0 × 1.0 mm | 5 / 7 / 10 |
| 2000 W | 3.7 × 1.9 mm (≈ "3 mm" in the notes?) | 9 / 12 / 19 |

Pariona's measured pool (680 × 257 µm) is **only 1–3 cells deep** on the
prescribed meshes. A 200–100 µm study can only say something meaningful if
the pool is millimetre-scale, i.e. ~1–2 kW absorbed (or a less conductive
material). The "≈3 mm" in the TF notes may well be the intended pool size.
**This must be confirmed with the TF** (power, absorptivity, material).

## 8. Open questions for the TF

1. Material: pure Al (Farzadi props, current default), Al-1.5Fe (Pariona),
   or another alloy?
2. Laser power, absorptivity, beam radius (and whether the radius is 1/e or
   1/e²), and scan speed. The target pool size should be ~3 mm?
3. Confirm that "B = 1e-4" and "Aroush ≈ 1e8" are B and A_mush from the
   Carman–Kozeny term, and whether AdditiveFOAM's built-in form is
   acceptable, or the exact Farzadi form should be coded.
4. ROI definition (5 × 5 mm in the full cross-section?) and domain length.
5. Values of h (top, sides, bottom/workbench), emissivity, and initial
   temperature.
6. Which output times and quantities to compare across meshes.
